# Model and exact copy thresholds

How many copies of a noisy unknown qubit are needed before stabilizer processing can improve its approximation to the original pure state? The answer depends on whether a protocol may declare failure. Four inputs suffice with heralding, whereas an output on every run requires five. The lower bounds hold for a larger class than the physical circuits used to attain them.

This page states the task and theorem. The [constructions](constructions.md), [heralded converse](heralded-converse.md) and [deterministic converse](deterministic-converse.md) supply its proofs.

## Inputs and fidelity

Let $\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^3$ be a unit vector and write

$$
\psi_{\mathbf u}=\frac{I+\mathbf u\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathbf u}(r)=\frac{I+r\mathbf u\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2},
\qquad 0<r<1.
$$

Here $\boldsymbol\sigma=(X,Y,Z)$ contains the Pauli matrices. The pure target direction $\mathbf u$ is unknown. The input is $\rho_{\mathbf u}(r)^{\otimes n}$: the noise acts independently on identically prepared copies. The retained Bloch polarization is $r$; the depolarizing replacement parameter is $\delta=1-r$, with $\mathcal D_\delta(\rho)=(1-\delta)\rho+\delta I/2$. The map may be designed for a fixed known $r$, but it cannot depend on $\mathbf u$.

An accepted operation is a completely positive, trace-nonincreasing linear map $\mathcal E$ from $n$ qubits to one. Define

$$
p_{\mathbf u}=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E\!\left(\rho_{\mathbf u}(r)^{\otimes n}\right),
\qquad
F_{\mathcal E,\mathbf u}
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\psi_{\mathbf u}\mathcal E(\rho_{\mathbf u}(r)^{\otimes n})\right]}{p_{\mathbf u}}.
$$

Fidelity here means the overlap with the pure target, equivalently the **square** of root Uhlmann fidelity. Returning one input has fidelity

$$
F_0(r)=\frac{1+r}{2}.
$$

The output and its fidelity average the protocol's classical choices and accepted outcomes, for each fixed input direction. Universal strict improvement means $F_{\mathcal E,\mathbf u}>F_0(r)$ for every unit vector $\mathbf u$. It does not require each individual measurement outcome to improve the score. Since the input is positive definite for $r<1$, every nonzero accepted CP map has $p_{\mathbf u}>0$ in this domain.

For comparison with the prior purification problem, use normalized uniform measure $d\mathbf u$ on the sphere and define

$$
\overline p=\int p_{\mathbf u}\,d\mathbf u,
\qquad
F_{\mathcal E}^{\mathrm H}(r)
=\frac{\int p_{\mathbf u}F_{\mathcal E,\mathbf u}\,d\mathbf u}{\overline p}.
$$

This is the fidelity of the accepted data, weighted by success. It is generally different from the unweighted integral of conditional fidelity. A root fidelity cannot be substituted inside either average. Success may depend on the direction; only its Haar average is prescribed. Universal pointwise improvement implies improvement in $F^{\mathrm H}$, so an upper bound on this averaged score supplies a converse for the stronger pointwise task.

## Allowed processing and the comparison cone

The attaining protocols use ordinary stabilizer processing: Clifford unitaries, stabilizer-state preparation, Pauli measurements, classical randomness and feedforward, discarding, and selection of accepted outcomes. Their quantum operations are ideal. No nonstabilizer ancillary resource is supplied.

For a converse, allow every accepted map whose unnormalized Choi operator lies in the cone generated by pure stabilizer-state projectors. With the input system first and output last, the convention is

$$
J_{\mathcal E}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^n-1}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\mathcal E(|i\rangle\langle j|),
\qquad
\mathcal E(A)=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{in}}\!\left[J_{\mathcal E}(A^T\otimes I)\right].
$$

The physical constraints are

$$
J_{\mathcal E}\ge0,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{out}}J_{\mathcal E}\le I_{2^n}.
$$

Equality in the second relation means trace preservation. For a nonzero map, $j=J_{\mathcal E}/\operatorname{tr}J_{\mathcal E}$ is a density operator in the stabilizer convex hull. In the trace-preserving case, $\operatorname{tr}J_{\mathcal E}=2^n$ and the input marginal of $j$ is $I_{2^n}/2^n$.

This Choi-cone condition characterizes completely stabilizer-preserving maps in the natural subnormalized sense. Necessity follows by applying the map to halves of stabilizer Bell pairs. Sufficiency follows by linking its Choi state to a stabilizer input through Bell projections; a positive rescaling does not affect cone membership. The [heralded converse](heralded-converse.md) makes this argument explicit. All ordinary stabilizer instruments satisfy the condition, while the theorem does not assume that every map in the larger cone has such an implementation.

The channel Choi-state characterization was established by [Seddon and Campbell (2019), Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03322). The Bell-projection argument extends it to the unequal input/output dimensions and subnormalized maps used here.

## Main theorem

Define the minimum copy count for *onset* to be the smallest number of inputs for which strict improvement for every direction is possible at some $r\in(0,1)$. Write

$$
r_*:=\sqrt{-7+2\sqrt{13}}\approx0.4594589763.
$$

**Theorem.** For the model above:

1. For every $n\le3$, every $0<r<1$, and every allowed nonzero accepted map,
   $F_{\mathcal E}^{\mathrm H}(r)\le F_0(r)$. Returning one input, followed by an independent acceptance coin, attains equality at any prescribed $0<\overline p\le1$.
2. For every $n\le4$, every $0<r<1$, and every allowed trace-preserving map, the same upper bound holds. Returning one input attains it.
3. An ordinary four-copy stabilizer instrument strictly improves fidelity for every direction throughout $0<r<r_*$, with $p_{\mathbf u}\ge1/8$. For a fixed $r$ in this interval, a variant attains strict pointwise gain at any prescribed Haar-average success $0<\overline p<1$.
4. An ordinary five-copy trace-preserving stabilizer protocol strictly improves fidelity for every direction on the same interval.

Consequently, the exact onset is **four copies with heralding and five deterministically**. In terms of prescribed average success, it is four for every value strictly between zero and one, and five at one. The sufficient interval refers to the displayed constructions; the converse assertions cover the whole noisy-input domain.

### Why average success one is deterministic

Let $A=I-\mathcal E^\dagger(I)\ge0$ be the rejection effect. If $\overline p=1$, then

$$
\operatorname{tr}\!\left[A\int\rho_{\mathbf u}(r)^{\otimes n}\,d\mathbf u\right]=0.
$$

The averaged input is positive definite for $r<1$. Thus $A=0$ and $\mathcal E$ is trace preserving. Fixing only average success creates no exception at the probability-one endpoint.

## Scope of the threshold

The theorem identifies the first copy count at which a fidelity gain exists. It does not claim that four or five inputs work at every polarization, that the attaining maps maximize fidelity for a fixed success, or that iteration gives arbitrarily pure outputs. All copies are counted per attempted batch. Repeated attempts and the fifth fallback input must be charged when comparing resource costs; see [physical interpretation](interpretation.md).

The fixed-axis unanimity filter is inherited from [Gendra et al. (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5479). The two-copy converse was proved in [He et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10516). The present sharp threshold uses the three-copy accepted-map converse and the four-copy deterministic converse together with the explicit stabilizer constructions. The [related-work account](../references/related-work.md) separates these ingredients and the relevant definitions.
